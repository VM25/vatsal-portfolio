Market making · 2026

The Liquidity Paradox

A market maker earns the gap between its buy and sell quotes, and loses every time the trader on the other side knows where the price is going. Can a five-second forecast tip that balance?

0trades
+0spread earned
−0lost to informed flow
0random moves on uninformed trades
0net, in ticks

+0 − 0 + 0 = 0

Illustration only: a simulated market maker quoting one tick either side of a price that moves two ticks at a time. In this toy, 55% of trades come from traders who know the next move (amber dots), so each of them costs more than the spread earns. On the other 45%, the next move is as likely to help as to hurt, so that line drifts around zero. Net = spread earned − informed loss + random moves. Not the study's data.

4.2% skill

On twenty sessions of SOL/USDT order-book data, the five-second forecast worked: an out-of-sample R² of 4.2% on the held-out sessions, positive in all five.

Simple quoting rules still lost money in every modeled scenario: about 1 to 2 bp of notional before fees, mostly to better-informed flow, and about 11 to 12 bp at the exchange's default fee tier.

Modeled historical replay: the orders never existed; fills are inferred under stated queue, latency and fee assumptions.

The hedge that stopped working

For most of the period from the late 1990s through 2020, U.S. stocks and Treasuries did not fall together. In 2022 they did.

Stock–Bond Diversification in an Inflation-Driven Rates Regime asks whether acting on that helps in practice, using only lagged, observable signals with realistic trading costs and position limits, and then measures why the hedge failed. Three findings, each with its evidence:

A breakeven is inflation compensation read off nominal minus TIPS yields. It is not a pure measure of expected inflation: it also carries an inflation risk premium and, in stressed markets, TIPS liquidity effects.

Now · FE800, Fall 2026

Testing whether real-rate dominance is a general feature of Treasury hedge failures or specific to 2022, by applying one standardized hedge book across other market-stress episodes.
